AIQ ETF Review: Global X's $8.8 Billion AI and Tech Fund

AIQ is Global X's biggest AI ETF: about 88 stocks from Palantir to SpaceX for 0.68% a year. What it owns, how it has done, and how it differs from BOTZ.

By Benjie Siegel · Published November 25, 2026 · Updated November 25, 2026 · 11 min read

Key takeaways

  • $AIQ is the Global X Artificial Intelligence & Technology ETF. It launched May 11, 2018, tracks the Indxx Artificial Intelligence & Big Data Index and charges 0.68% a year.
  • It is an AI and big tech fund, not a robotics fund. On November 24, 2026 its top holdings were Palantir, Microsoft, Tesla, Apple, Amazon and SpaceX, and about 73% of the fund was in information technology.
  • Global X reported $8.76 billion in net assets and 88 holdings as of November 24, 2026, making it much bigger than its robotics sister fund $BOTZ.
  • Through October 31, 2026 it returned 13.80% over one year, 32.38% a year over three years and 14.01% a year over five years at NAV, with a beta of 1.99 against the S&P 500.
  • It barely pays anything: a 30-day SEC yield of -0.07% and a June 2026 distribution of less than a tenth of a cent per share.

$AIQ is the Global X Artificial Intelligence & Technology ETF, and the short answer to "what am I buying?" is this: about 88 of the biggest AI software, cloud and chip companies in the world, picked by an index and weighted mostly by size, for 0.68% a year. It is not a robot fund. It is the AI brain, not the robot body, and that is exactly why it is worth understanding before you put it next to a robotics ETF.

People land on this site searching for "AIQ ETF fees" and "Global X artificial intelligence ETF" almost as often as they search for the robot funds, so it gets the same deep dive I gave BOTZ, ROBO, ARKQ and FBOT. I went through Global X's fund page, holdings file, performance and risk numbers as of November 24, 2026. Here is what I found.

The basics that matter most:

  • It is big. Global X reported $8.76 billion in net assets on November 24, 2026, several times the size of its robotics sister fund $BOTZ.
  • It is passive. It tracks the Indxx Artificial Intelligence & Big Data Index, which is reconstituted once a year.
  • It is global. The index has no sector or country limits, so you get Tencent, Samsung, SK hynix, SAP and Siemens next to the US giants.
  • It is liquid. Global X showed a 30-day median bid-ask spread of just 0.02%.
  • It is old for a theme fund. It launched May 11, 2018, so it has been through 2022's crash and the AI boom since.

How AIQ picks its stocks

This is the part most people skip, and it explains the whole portfolio. The Indxx index sorts eligible companies into two buckets:

  • AI applied to products and services, plus AI as a service for big data. Think software platforms, cloud providers, internet companies and anyone using AI at scale to sell something. The index takes up to the top 60 companies from this bucket.
  • AI hardware and quantum computing. The chip designers, memory makers and hardware companies that make AI possible. The index takes up to the top 25 from this bucket.

That is up to 85 companies, and Global X listed 88 holdings on November 24, 2026. Because the biggest companies in each bucket win, the result looks a lot like a global mega cap tech fund with an AI filter. Global X reported a weighted average market cap of about $984 billion. That is not a small cap innovation fund. That is the trillion dollar club.

Why this matters: With a robotics fund like $BOTZ, the index hunts for companies that build machines. With $AIQ, the index hunts for companies that use or enable AI, and in 2026 that describes most of big tech. So when you buy $AIQ, you are mostly making a bet on the largest tech platforms continuing to win, with a tilt toward chips and AI software.

What AIQ costs you

$AIQ charges 0.68% a year. The average across the 30 robotics and AI funds we track is 0.66%. In dollars, $10,000 in $AIQ costs about $68 a year. The same $10,000 costs about $68 in $BOTZ or $THNQ, about $47 in iShares' $ARTY and about $95 in $ROBO.

Here is how I think about it. $AIQ is not cheap for a fund that mostly owns the biggest tech stocks in the world. You could get Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, Meta and Alphabet in a broad index fund for a few basis points. What the 0.68% really buys you is the AI filter, the international chip names and the equal footing for Palantir, Oracle and the memory makers. Whether that is worth roughly 10 to 20 times the cost of a plain index fund is the real question. My cheapest robotics ETFs guide ranks every option by cost.

Three things stand out. First, the three year number is huge. More than 32% a year is what the AI boom looked like from the inside. Second, the five year number is much lower at about 14%, because that window includes 2022, when growth and tech stocks got crushed. Third, the fund trails its index by about 0.6 to 0.9 points a year, which is roughly the fee plus trading costs. That is normal for an index fund, and it is why the fee matters.

The ride is not smooth. Global X shows a beta of 1.99 against the S&P 500, 1.39 against the Nasdaq-100, and a standard deviation of 29.70%. And November 2026 is a live example: StockAnalysis showed $AIQ down about 13.7% over the past month, with the price at $53.74 on November 24, against a 52-week range of $44.46 to $70.26. It was still up about 5.7% for the year to date.

My take: A fund that moves about twice as hard as the S&P 500 can feel amazing for three years and awful for three months. If a 25% to 35% drop would make you sell, size it smaller. The worst outcome with a theme fund is buying it after a great run and selling it after the first bad quarter.

Does AIQ pay a dividend?

Barely. $AIQ pays semi-annually, but the June 2026 distribution was $0.00045 a share, the December 2025 payment was about $0.045, and Global X showed a 30-day SEC yield of -0.07% on November 24, 2026. That negative number just means the fund's tiny dividend income does not cover its 0.68% fee. The next scheduled ex-date is December 30, 2026.

As an income investor, this is the part I always flag. Nothing in this fund pays you to wait. If you want cash flow, you need something else doing that job, which is what my sister site TopDividendETFs.com covers. For the robotics funds that do pay, see my robotics ETF dividends guide.

How AIQ ranks by size

$AIQ is the biggest fund on this site by a wide margin. Size matters for two reasons. Closure risk is close to zero, and trading costs are tiny, with a 0.02% median spread. Global X also showed the fund trading at a discount to NAV on 23 days and a premium on 16 days so far this quarter, with the biggest gap under 1%. That is tight. You can trade it like a big index fund.

The real risks

Mega cap tech concentration

About 85% of the fund sits in tech and internet companies. If you already own an S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 fund, a large chunk of $AIQ is stuff you own already: Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, Meta and Alphabet together are about 17% of it.

AI spending cycle

Chips, memory and cloud names make money when companies keep spending heavily on AI data centers. If that spending slows, the hardware bucket (AMD, Broadcom, TSMC, Samsung, SK hynix, Micron, Nvidia) can fall fast. Several of those names dropped 8% to 11% in a single day in late November 2026.

Volatility

A beta near 2 against the S&P 500 and a 29.70% standard deviation tell you what a bad year looks like. Plan for it before it happens.

Foreign exposure

Tencent, Alibaba, Samsung, SK hynix, SAP and Siemens bring currency risk, and the Chinese names bring regulatory and political risk on top.

Theme drift

"AI company" is a wide label. Netflix and Apple are great businesses, but they are not what most people picture when they buy an AI fund. You are trusting the index's definition, not your own.

Who AIQ fits, and who it does not

$AIQ may fit you if you want one big, liquid, index based fund for the whole AI stack, from software to chips, you are fine with mega cap tech at the core, and you are keeping it to a small slice of your portfolio.

$AIQ probably does not fit you if you want robot and automation makers (look at $BOTZ, $ROBO or $IBOT, or my best robotics ETFs guide), you want humanoid robot exposure (see my humanoid robot ETFs guide), you already hold a lot of Nasdaq-100, or you need any income at all.

How I would actually use AIQ

If I owned $AIQ, it would sit next to a broad index fund as a tilt, not replace one. Before buying, I would add up how much Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, Meta and Alphabet I already own across all my funds, because that is where the overlap hides.

If what you really want is the physical side of AI, robots in factories, warehouses and hospitals, I would rather own a dedicated robotics fund and let my core index fund handle the software giants. If what you want is the AI build-out itself, $AIQ is one of the cleanest single tickers for it, with the international chip makers most US funds leave out.

And I would judge it once a year against two things: the Nasdaq-100 and a cheaper AI option like $ARTY. If $AIQ is not beating plain big tech over rolling three year periods, the AI filter is not earning its fee.

My takeaway

$AIQ is Global X's flagship AI fund: about 88 stocks, $8.76 billion in assets, a 0.68% fee and a portfolio built around Palantir, Microsoft, Tesla, Apple, Amazon and SpaceX, with the world's big chip makers just behind. It has been a great fund over three years and a decent one over five, with about twice the market's swings.

Just do not buy it thinking you own the robot makers. That is $BOTZ's job. I'd rather pick the one that matches what I actually believe in, AI software and chips or physical robots, than own both by accident.

Frequently asked questions

What is the AIQ ETF expense ratio?

AIQ charges 0.68% a year, or about $68 a year on a $10,000 investment. That is the same as its sister fund BOTZ and the ROBO Global AI fund THNQ, more than iShares' ARTY at 0.47%, and less than ROBO at 0.95%.

What does AIQ hold?

About 88 stocks as of November 24, 2026. The largest were Palantir (4.25%), Microsoft (4.23%), Tesla (3.91%), Apple (3.58%), Amazon (3.51%), SpaceX (3.46%), Meta (3.16%), Oracle (2.97%), Alphabet (2.86%) and Netflix (2.78%). Chip makers like AMD, Broadcom, TSMC, Samsung, SK hynix and Nvidia sit just below the top 10.

Is AIQ a robotics ETF?

Not really. AIQ is an AI and big data fund. It owns software platforms, cloud companies and chip makers, and only a few names with a real robotics angle, like Tesla and Siemens. If you want robot and automation makers, BOTZ, ROBO or IBOT are more direct.

Does AIQ pay a dividend?

Barely. AIQ pays twice a year, but the June 2026 distribution was $0.00045 a share and the December 2025 payment was about $0.045. Global X showed a 30-day SEC yield of -0.07% on November 24, 2026. It is a growth fund, not an income fund.

Is AIQ a good investment?

That depends on what you want. AIQ gives you a big, liquid, index based basket of AI leaders for 0.68%, with strong three year returns. But it is concentrated in mega cap tech you may already own and it swings about twice as hard as the S&P 500. It fits best as a small slice next to a broad index fund. This is not a recommendation.
