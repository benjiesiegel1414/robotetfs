The cheapest fund on the list is $BOTT at 0.35% a year. The most expensive non-leveraged funds charge close to three times as much.

What the fee gap really costs

On a $10,000 investment, a 0.35% fund costs about $35 a year. A 0.95% fund costs about $95 a year. Over ten years, with growth, that difference can add up to well over $600, before counting any difference in performance.

Why the cheapest fund is not automatically the best

Size matters. Some of the lowest-cost funds are also among the newest and smallest. Small funds can have wider bid-ask spreads, which can cost more than the fee savings if you trade often.

Strategy matters more. A humanoid fund and an industrial automation fund are different bets. Picking the cheapest one only makes sense after you have decided what you want to own.

Big funds are often cheap enough. $BOTZ charges 0.68% with $3.10B in assets, and $IBOT charges 0.47%. Large, established funds tend to trade more easily.

The bottom line

Use fees as a tiebreaker between funds that own what you want, not as the first filter. See the full robotics ETF table to sort by fees, assets and yield, or line up two funds on the compare page. Nothing here is a recommendation to buy or sell.

Frequently asked questions

What is the cheapest robotics ETF?

The fund at the top of the table above has the lowest expense ratio on our list. Fees can change, so the table updates with our data.

Do cheaper robotics ETFs perform better?

Lower fees help every year, but strategy and holdings matter more for returns. A cheap fund that owns the wrong mix can still trail.

Why do some robotics ETFs charge more?

Actively managed funds, smaller funds and specialized strategies usually charge more than large index funds.
