Robotics ETF FAQ

What is a robotics ETF?

A robotics ETF is a fund that holds companies building robots and automation: industrial robot makers, motion and sensor suppliers, surgical robotics, humanoid robot developers and the AI chips and software that power them.

How many robotics ETFs are there?

We currently track 25 US-listed ETFs focused on robotics, humanoids, physical AI, autonomous vehicles and drones, holding about $17.6B combined.

What is the largest robotics ETF?

AIQ (Global X Artificial Intelligence & Technology ETF) is the largest fund on our list with about $9.10B in assets. Among pure robotics funds, BOTZ is the biggest.

What is the cheapest robotics ETF?

BOTT has the lowest expense ratio on our list at 0.35% a year.

Is there a humanoid robot ETF?

Yes. There are now 7 humanoid-focused ETFs on our list, including KOID, HUMN and BOTT. See our humanoid robot ETF page for the full list.

Do robotics ETFs pay dividends?

Most pay small dividends, usually under 1% a year, and many pay only once or twice a year. These are growth funds, not income funds. Check the yield and payout schedule columns in the table.
