Drone and Robotaxi ETFs: Investing in Machines That Move on Their Own

Self-driving cars and drones are robotics on the move. Here are the ETFs focused on autonomy, what they hold and how they differ from classic robotics funds.

Not every robot works in a factory. Some fly and some drive. Drones and self-driving vehicles are two of the fastest-moving parts of robotics, and a small group of ETFs now targets them directly.

Robotaxis: $CABZ

Roundhill's $CABZ targets companies building autonomous vehicles and robotaxi services, from vehicle platforms to the sensors, chips and software behind them. It launched on January 14, 2026 and is still small at $3.1M.

Drones: $DRNZ

REX's $DRNZ focuses on drone and unmanned systems makers. Many of these companies sell heavily to militaries, so contract wins and defense budgets can move the fund. It holds $78M.

The mix: $WDRN and $ARKQ

WisdomTree's $WDRN combines drones with humanoids and other physical AI. ARK's $ARKQ, launched back in 2014, is an actively managed fund covering autonomous vehicles, robotics, energy storage and space, run in ARK's concentrated style.

How autonomy funds differ from classic robotics funds

Classic robotics funds like $BOTZ and $ROBO lean on factory automation, industrial robots and AI chips. Autonomy funds are narrower and more tied to a few big themes: regulation of self-driving cars, defense spending and the pace of commercial drone adoption. Expect bigger swings.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a drone ETF?

Yes. DRNZ from REX Shares focuses on drone and unmanned systems companies, and WDRN mixes drones with humanoids and physical AI.

Is there a robotaxi ETF?

CABZ from Roundhill targets robotaxi and autonomous vehicle companies. ARKQ also holds autonomy and robotics names.

Are drone ETFs defense funds?

Partly. Many drone makers depend on defense contracts, so drone ETFs can behave like defense funds.
