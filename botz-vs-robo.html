BOTZ vs ROBO: The Two Original Robotics ETFs Compared

BOTZ and ROBO are the two names most investors find first. Here is how they differ on size, fees, weighting and what they actually own, with live data.

If you search for a robotics ETF, two tickers show up before anything else: $BOTZ from Global X and $ROBO from ROBO Global. They are the veterans of the category, and together they hold more money than most of the newer robotics funds combined. But they are built in very different ways.

The numbers side by side

$BOTZ holds $3.10B and charges 0.68% a year. $ROBO holds $1.90B and charges 0.95%. On a $10,000 position, that fee gap works out to roughly $27 a year in favor of BOTZ.

Difference #1: concentration

BOTZ is the more concentrated fund. A relatively small group of large companies, including big AI chip and factory automation names, makes up a large share of the portfolio. When those leaders run, BOTZ tends to follow them closely.

ROBO spreads its money more evenly across a longer list of companies, including many small and mid-size robotics, sensing and motion control businesses around the world. That makes it more diversified across the robotics supply chain, but it also means it can lag in years when a handful of mega-cap AI stocks drive the market.

Difference #2: age and track record

ROBO launched on October 22, 2013, making it the oldest robotics ETF still trading. BOTZ followed on September 12, 2016. Both have been through full market cycles, which is more than most of the humanoid and physical AI funds can say.

Difference #3: income

Neither is an income fund. $BOTZ yields about 0.53% and $ROBO about 0.38%. If dividends matter to you, robotics is not the place to look for them.

Which one fits

If you want fewer, bigger names and a lower fee, BOTZ is the more focused bet. If you want broad exposure to the whole robotics supply chain, including smaller companies, ROBO is built for that. Some investors look at both and pick based on how much single-stock concentration they are comfortable with.
