The Best Robotics ETFs of 2026, Compared

Every US-listed robotics ETF ranked by size, with live assets, fees and yields, and a plain-English look at what each fund is really betting on.

By Benjie Siegel · Published November 6, 2026 · Updated November 27, 2026 · 15 min read

For most of the last decade, investors who wanted robotics exposure had two main choices: BOTZ and ROBO. That changed fast. Humanoid robots, physical AI and robotaxis pulled in a wave of new launches, and there are now 30 robotics-focused ETFs on our list holding a combined $19.5B.

They are not all the same bet. Below is every fund we track, ranked by assets, with live numbers and what makes each one different. This list is for research, not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.

The average expense ratio across the group is 0.66%. The cheapest is $BOTT at 0.35%, the oldest is $ROBO, and the newest launch is $RBTX, which started trading on October 20, 2026.

Each fund, ranked by assets

1. $AIQ: Global X Artificial Intelligence & Technology ETF

Global X · Index · $8.40B in assets · 0.68% expense ratio · launched May 11, 2018

Global X's AI and technology ETF is the largest fund on our list. It tracks companies developing and using AI, including the chip, cloud and software giants plus robotics and automation names.

Worth knowing: This is a broad AI fund rather than a pure robotics fund. It holds many of the same mega-cap tech stocks found in a Nasdaq index fund.

Full $AIQ profile and live price →

2. $ARTY: iShares Future AI & Tech ETF

iShares (BlackRock) · Index · $2.90B in assets · 0.47% expense ratio · launched June 26, 2018

The iShares Future AI & Tech ETF tracks the Morningstar Global Artificial Intelligence Select Index. It used to trade as IRBO, the iShares Robotics and AI fund, before its mandate changed in 2024. Today it is mostly AI chips, cloud and software, led by names such as AMD, Broadcom and TSMC.

Worth knowing: This is an AI fund now, not a robotics fund. Do not expect much industrial robot or humanoid exposure. We list it because many investors still look for it under its old robotics name.

Full $ARTY profile and live price →

3. $BOTZ: Global X Robotics & Artificial Intelligence ETF

Global X · Index · $2.60B in assets · 0.68% expense ratio · launched September 12, 2016

The largest and best-known robotics ETF. BOTZ tracks an index of companies that make industrial robots, automation systems, surgical robots and the AI software and chips behind them, with heavy weightings in the US and Japan.

Worth knowing: It is fairly concentrated, so a handful of large AI chip and automation names drive a big share of returns. Overlap with broad tech funds is meaningful.

Full $BOTZ profile and live price →

4. $ROBO: ROBO Global Robotics & Automation Index ETF

Exchange Traded Concepts (ROBO Global) · Index · $1.60B in assets · 0.95% expense ratio · launched October 22, 2013

The original robotics ETF, launched in 2013. ROBO tracks the ROBO Global Robotics and Automation Index, which spreads money across a wide mix of robotics, sensing, motion control and automation companies around the world.

Worth knowing: Its holdings are more equally spread than most peers, which means more small and mid-size companies. That diversifies the bet but also means it can lag when a few mega-cap AI names lead the market. It carries one of the higher fees in the group.

Full $ROBO profile and live price →

5. $ARKQ: ARK Autonomous Technology & Robotics ETF

ARK Invest · Active · $1.60B in assets · 0.75% expense ratio · launched September 30, 2014

ARK's autonomous technology and robotics fund is an actively managed bet on autonomous vehicles, robotics, 3D printing, energy storage and space exploration, run in ARK's high-conviction style.

Worth knowing: Holdings are concentrated and can include large positions in a few names like electric vehicle and autonomy companies. It behaves more like a growth and disruption fund than a pure robotics index.

Full $ARKQ profile and live price →

6. $ROBT: First Trust Nasdaq Artificial Intelligence and Robotics ETF

First Trust · Index · $610M in assets · 0.65% expense ratio · launched February 21, 2018

First Trust's Nasdaq AI and robotics ETF tracks companies classified as AI and robotics enablers, engagers and enhancers, with a fairly equal-weighted approach.

Worth knowing: Its equal weighting spreads money across many mid-size names. That limits mega-cap concentration but can lag when the biggest AI stocks lead.

Full $ROBT profile and live price →

7. $THNQ: ROBO Global Artificial Intelligence ETF

Exchange Traded Concepts (ROBO Global) · Index · $359M in assets · 0.68% expense ratio · launched May 11, 2020

ROBO Global's AI ETF tracks companies across the AI value chain, from chips and cloud infrastructure to AI software and robotics applications.

Worth knowing: A companion fund to ROBO with more of a software and infrastructure tilt. Check overlap if you own both.

Full $THNQ profile and live price →

8. $KOID: KraneShares Global Humanoid Robotics and Physical AI Index ETF

KraneShares · Index · $283M in assets · 0.69% expense ratio · launched June 4, 2025

KraneShares' humanoid robotics fund tracks companies building humanoid robots and the parts inside them: actuators, sensors, reducers, batteries and AI chips. It has grown into one of the largest dedicated humanoid ETFs.

Worth knowing: Humanoid robots are an early-stage market. Many holdings are component suppliers whose revenue today comes mostly from other businesses, and there is meaningful exposure to Chinese and other Asian companies.

Full $KOID profile and live price →

9. $DRIV: Global X Autonomous & Electric Vehicles ETF

Global X · Index · $255M in assets · 0.68% expense ratio · launched April 13, 2018

The Global X Autonomous & Electric Vehicles ETF tracks the Solactive Autonomous & Electric Vehicles Index: about 75 companies that build self-driving and electric vehicles or the chips, software and materials behind them. Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla have been among the largest positions.

Worth knowing: A big part of the fund is electric vehicles, batteries and large tech companies, so it is a broad way to own autonomy rather than a pure robotaxi bet. It overlaps with general tech funds.

Full $DRIV profile and live price →

10. $FBOT: Fidelity Disruptive Automation ETF

Fidelity · Active · $159M in assets · 0.50% expense ratio · launched April 16, 2020

Fidelity's disruptive automation fund is actively managed and invests in companies that design and use robotics, automation and related technologies across industries.

Worth knowing: One of the few funds here that pays quarterly. As an active fund, its holdings can shift, so check the latest portfolio on Fidelity's site.

Innovator's Deepwater frontier tech ETF tracks a concentrated list of frontier technology companies chosen by Deepwater Asset Management, covering AI, robotics, autonomy, AR and VR.

Worth knowing: Concentrated and volatile. Robotics is one of several frontier themes inside it, not the only focus.

The iShares Self-Driving EV and Tech ETF tracks the NYSE FactSet Global Autonomous Driving and Electric Vehicle Index. It holds about 70 automakers, battery makers and autonomous driving suppliers from around the world.

Worth knowing: Battery makers such as Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution carry heavy weights, so the fund often trades with the electric vehicle cycle more than with self-driving news. It pays one of the higher yields on this list.

Worth knowing: This is as much a defense and space fund as a robotics fund, and it is concentrated: the top ten are about half the portfolio. Many holdings are small, unprofitable companies that swing hard. It carries one of the highest fees on the list.

How to choose between them

Decide how pure you want it. Funds like $BOTZ, $ROBO and $IBOT stay close to robotics and automation (my ROBO ETF review covers the oldest of them in depth, and my IBOT ETF review covers the cheapest core robotics fund). If you would rather have a manager picking the stocks, my FBOT ETF review covers Fidelity's actively managed automation fund. Funds like $AIQ and $THNQ are broader AI funds where robotics is one slice next to chips, cloud and software.

Decide how early you want to be. Humanoid funds like $KOID, $HUMN and $BOTT bet on a market that barely exists yet. Many of their holdings are component makers whose revenue still comes mostly from cars, factories and electronics.

Check size and trading. Many of the newest funds hold only a few million dollars. Small funds can have wide bid-ask spreads and a higher chance of closing.

Watch the overlap. Big AI chip and automation names show up in many of these funds. Owning three robotics ETFs may not be as diversified as it looks.

Know what leverage does. $UBOT seeks 2X the daily return of its index and resets daily. It is a trading tool, not a long-term holding for most investors.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best robotics ETF?

There is no single best fund. They differ on focus (industrial automation vs. humanoids vs. broad AI), management style, fees and size. Compare them on those points and match them to what you want to own. Nothing here is a recommendation.

What is the largest robotics ETF?

BOTZ is the largest pure robotics ETF. AIQ is bigger, but it is a broad AI fund that includes robotics rather than a robotics fund. Assets change daily, so check the live table.

What is the oldest robotics ETF?

ROBO launched in October 2013 and is the oldest robotics ETF still trading.

Are robotics ETFs good for income?

Not really. Most yield under 1% and pay once or twice a year. They are growth funds.
