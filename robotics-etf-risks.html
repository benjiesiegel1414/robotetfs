Robotics ETF Risks: 7 Things to Know Before You Buy

Robotics is one of the most exciting themes in the market, which is exactly why the risks deserve a clear look. Here are seven to understand before buying any robotics ETF.

By Benjie Siegel · November 7, 2026 · Live data

Robots are moving into warehouses, hospitals, farms and homes, and robotics ETFs are a simple way to invest in that trend. But theme investing has its own risks. Here are seven worth understanding before you buy any of the 25 funds we track.

1. Hype can run ahead of revenue

Humanoid robots get a lot of attention, but very few companies sell them in volume today. Many humanoid ETF holdings are component makers whose revenue still comes mostly from cars, factories and electronics. Prices can move on excitement long before sales show up.

2. Concentration

Some funds put a big share of their money into a handful of large companies. That can help in good years and hurt in bad ones. $BOTZ is more concentrated, while $ROBO spreads money more evenly.

3. Overlap with what you already own

Large AI chip and automation names show up in many robotics funds and in broad tech funds. If you already own a Nasdaq index fund, a robotics ETF may add less diversification than you expect. Broad AI funds like $AIQ overlap even more.

4. Small, young funds

Many robotics ETFs launched in 2025 and 2026 and hold only a few million dollars. Small funds can have wide trading spreads and can close if they do not grow. See our new robotics ETFs guide.

5. Country and currency risk

Robotics leaders are spread across Japan, China, South Korea, Taiwan and Europe. Funds like $CROB focus on a single country, which adds political and currency risk.

6. Leverage

$UBOT seeks 2X the daily return of a robotics index. Leveraged funds reset daily and can drift far from 2X over longer periods. They are built for short-term trading.

7. Fees and almost no income

The average fee on our list is 0.67%, and most robotics funds yield under 1%. These are growth bets, so you are relying on price gains, not dividends. See the cheapest robotics ETFs.

The other side

None of this means robotics funds are bad investments. They give simple, diversified access to a long-term trend that is hard to invest in through single stocks. The point is to know what you are buying. Compare funds on the robotics ETF table, read the prospectus and consider a licensed professional. Nothing here is a recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

Are robotics ETFs risky?

They are growth funds tied to a theme, so they can swing more than broad market funds. Many newer funds are also small and concentrated.

Do robotics ETFs overlap with tech ETFs?

Often, yes. Large AI chip and automation companies appear in many robotics ETFs and also in big tech index funds.

Are robotics ETFs good long-term investments?

That depends on your goals and risk tolerance. We do not make recommendations. Read each fund's prospectus and consider a licensed professional.
