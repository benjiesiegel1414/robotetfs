What counts as core robotics & automation

These are the classic robotics funds. They own the companies that build industrial robot arms, warehouse automation, motion control, machine vision and surgical robots, along with the chips and software that run them.

Core robotics funds are the most established part of this list, with the longest track records and the most assets. They differ mainly in how they weight their holdings: some lean on a few large companies, others spread money evenly across 70 or more.

Each fund in plain English

1. $BOTZ: Global X Robotics & Artificial Intelligence ETF

Global X · Index · $2.90B in assets · 0.68% expense ratio · launched September 12, 2016

The largest and best-known robotics ETF. BOTZ tracks an index of companies that make industrial robots, automation systems, surgical robots and the AI software and chips behind them, with heavy weightings in the US and Japan.

Worth knowing: It is fairly concentrated, so a handful of large AI chip and automation names drive a big share of returns. Overlap with broad tech funds is meaningful.

Full $BOTZ profile and live price →

2. $ROBO: ROBO Global Robotics & Automation Index ETF

Exchange Traded Concepts (ROBO Global) · Index · $1.80B in assets · 0.95% expense ratio · launched October 22, 2013

The original robotics ETF, launched in 2013. ROBO tracks the ROBO Global Robotics and Automation Index, which spreads money across a wide mix of robotics, sensing, motion control and automation companies around the world.

Worth knowing: Its holdings are more equally spread than most peers, which means more small and mid-size companies. That diversifies the bet but also means it can lag when a few mega-cap AI names lead the market. It carries one of the higher fees in the group.

Full $ROBO profile and live price →

3. $ARKQ: ARK Autonomous Technology & Robotics ETF

ARK Invest · Active · $1.70B in assets · 0.75% expense ratio · launched September 30, 2014

ARK's autonomous technology and robotics fund is an actively managed bet on autonomous vehicles, robotics, 3D printing, energy storage and space exploration, run in ARK's high-conviction style.

Worth knowing: Holdings are concentrated and can include large positions in a few names like electric vehicle and autonomy companies. It behaves more like a growth and disruption fund than a pure robotics index.

Full $ARKQ profile and live price →

4. $FBOT: Fidelity Disruptive Automation ETF

Fidelity · Active · $180M in assets · 0.50% expense ratio · launched April 16, 2020

Fidelity's disruptive automation fund is actively managed and invests in companies that design and use robotics, automation and related technologies across industries.

Worth knowing: One of the few funds here that pays quarterly. As an active fund, its holdings can shift, so check the latest portfolio on Fidelity's site.

Full $FBOT profile and live price →

5. $IBOT: VanEck Robotics ETF

VanEck · Index · $104M in assets · 0.47% expense ratio · launched April 5, 2023

VanEck's robotics ETF tracks companies that earn a large share of revenue from robotics, including industrial and collaborative robots, the software that runs them and key enabling technologies.

Worth knowing: Its revenue-based screen keeps it closer to pure robotics names than broad AI funds. It is smaller than the category leaders.

Full $IBOT profile and live price →

6. $RBTX: iShares Future Robotics and Related Technologies Active ETF

iShares (BlackRock) · Active · $14M in assets · 0.65% expense ratio · launched October 20, 2026

iShares' future robotics fund is an actively managed BlackRock ETF focused on companies driving robotics and related technologies, from robot makers to the components and software around them.

Worth knowing: Very new, so it has almost no track record yet. It comes from the largest ETF issuer in the world, which often helps a new fund gather assets.

Full $RBTX profile and live price →

7. $RTOO: VistaShares Robotics Supercycle ETF

VistaShares · $3.2M in assets · 0.75% expense ratio · launched July 15, 2026

VistaShares' robotics supercycle ETF targets companies positioned to benefit from a long build-out of robotics across manufacturing, logistics and consumer markets.

Worth knowing: Launched in 2026 and still small. New, small funds can have wider spreads and a higher risk of closing if they do not gather assets.

Full $RTOO profile and live price →

8. $TORK: KraneShares Actuator ETF

KraneShares · $2.4M in assets · 0.65% expense ratio · launched September 23, 2026

KraneShares' actuator ETF focuses on one layer of the robot: the actuators, motors, reducers and motion parts that let robots move. It is a picks-and-shovels approach to robotics.

Worth knowing: A narrow and very new fund. Component suppliers often sell to many industries, so robotics may be only part of their revenue today.

Full $TORK profile and live price →

9. $ROBX: Amplify Top 10 Robotics ETF

Amplify ETFs · Active · $1.2M in assets · 0.49% expense ratio · launched August 18, 2026

Amplify's robotics fund holds a concentrated basket of about 10 robotics companies from around the world. It leans on the Bloomberg Top 10 Momentum Robotics Index for ideas but is actively managed and does not track it, with Samsung Asset Management as sub-adviser.

Worth knowing: Ten stocks is very concentrated, so one bad holding can move the whole fund. It launched in August 2026 and is still tiny, which can mean wide bid-ask spreads. Use limit orders and check assets before buying.
