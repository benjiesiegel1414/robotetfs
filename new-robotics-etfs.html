For most of the last decade, robotics investors had a short menu: $ROBO, $BOTZ, $ARKQ and a few broader AI funds. That changed fast. Starting in 2025, a wave of new funds arrived from KraneShares, Roundhill, Themes, Defiance, VistaShares, iShares, WisdomTree, Corgi, Tuttle and others, most of them built around one idea: humanoid robots and the physical AI that powers them.

This guide covers every new fund on our list: when it launched, what it targets, how big it has grown, and what to watch with young ETFs. All figures update daily from our data.

The bars for 2025 and 2026 tell the story. Robotics went from a niche theme with a handful of funds to one of the busiest launch categories in the ETF market. Our list now covers 25 funds, and the majority of them are less than two years old.

Why now: what is driving the launches

Humanoid robots went mainstream. Automakers, AI labs and startups began showing humanoid robots doing real work in factories and warehouses. Investor interest followed, and fund companies moved to give investors a way in.

AI moved into the physical world. After years of AI investing focused on chips and software, the next chapter is "physical AI": machines that see, decide and act. Robotics is the most direct way to express that idea.

Supply chain stories became investable. A humanoid robot needs dozens of precise actuators, reducers, sensors and batteries. Issuers realized they could build funds around those component makers, many of which are established public companies.

The ETF industry is competitive. Being first to a hot theme matters. Issuers often launch quickly and let the market decide which products survive.

Robot component funds

Two funds take a picks-and-shovels approach by focusing on the parts that make robots move. $TORK from KraneShares and $AT from Defiance both target actuators and motion components. The logic: whichever robot makers win, they all need precise motors, reducers and actuators.

Big-issuer entrants

$RBTX brings iShares, the largest ETF issuer in the world, into actively managed robotics investing. $PBOT from Pictet brings a long-time European thematic manager to US investors. Large issuers often have the distribution to help a new fund grow quickly.

Physical AI, drones and robotaxis

$WDRN from WisdomTree mixes humanoids and drones under a physical AI banner. $CABZ from Roundhill targets robotaxis and autonomous vehicles, and $DRNZ from REX focuses on drones. We cover these in depth in our drone and robotaxi ETF guide.

Broader robotics

$RTOO from VistaShares targets a long "supercycle" of robotics adoption across manufacturing, logistics and consumer markets.

How the new funds compare with the veterans

Despite all the launches, most of the money in robotics still sits in older funds. $BOTZ, $ROBO and $ARKQ each hold billions or close to it, while many 2026 launches hold under $20 million. Assets matter because they affect trading costs and the odds that a fund survives.

Six things to check before buying a new robotics ETF

  1. Assets under management. Funds with very little money are at higher risk of closing. There is no fixed cutoff, but funds that stay very small for a long time are the ones most likely to be shut down.
  2. Trading volume and spreads. Thinly traded funds can have wide bid-ask spreads. Use limit orders.
  3. Top holdings. Many humanoid funds own the same component makers. Owning three of them may not diversify you much.
  4. Geography. Humanoid supply chains run heavily through China, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan. That adds currency and political risk.
  5. Index vs. active. Index funds follow fixed rules. Active funds can change direction quickly. Read how each fund picks its holdings.
  6. Fees. New funds often launch with competitive fees, but compare them against established options. See our cheapest robotics ETFs ranking.
Remember: a new fund with a great story still has no track record. You are trusting the strategy and the issuer, not years of evidence.

What to expect next

Launch waves in hot themes usually follow a pattern: a rush of new funds, then consolidation as the market picks winners and the smallest funds close or merge. Expect more humanoid and physical AI funds to launch, and expect a few of today's smallest funds to disappear. We track every launch and closure on the robotics ETF table.

Frequently asked questions

What is the newest robotics ETF?

The fund at the top of the launch table above is the most recent launch on our list. We add new funds as they start trading.

Why are so many robotics ETFs launching?

Interest in humanoid robots, physical AI and robotaxis surged, and fund companies launched products to meet that demand. ETF issuers often race to be first to a hot theme.

Are new ETFs riskier than older ones?

They carry extra risks: no track record, small asset bases, wider trading spreads and a higher chance of closing. The underlying stocks may be just as volatile as in older funds.

What happens if a new ETF closes?

The fund announces a closing date, sells its holdings and pays shareholders the cash value. You do not lose the value of your shares, but a sale can create a taxable gain and you will need to reinvest elsewhere.
