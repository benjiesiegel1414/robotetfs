The short version

$KOID is cheaper to own, at 0.69% a year versus 0.95%. $ROBO is the bigger fund, with $1.80B in assets. $ROBO has been around longer, launching on October 22, 2013.

$KOID

KraneShares' humanoid robotics fund tracks companies building humanoid robots and the parts inside them: actuators, sensors, reducers, batteries and AI chips. It has grown into one of the largest dedicated humanoid ETFs. Humanoid robots are an early-stage market. Many holdings are component suppliers whose revenue today comes mostly from other businesses, and there is meaningful exposure to Chinese and other Asian companies.

$ROBO

The original robotics ETF, launched in 2013. ROBO tracks the ROBO Global Robotics and Automation Index, which spreads money across a wide mix of robotics, sensing, motion control and automation companies around the world. Its holdings are more equally spread than most peers, which means more small and mid-size companies. That diversifies the bet but also means it can lag when a few mega-cap AI names lead the market. It carries one of the higher fees in the group.

Lower fees and more assets are helpful, but they are not the whole story. The bigger question is which strategy matches what you want to own. This comparison is not a recommendation to buy or sell either fund.

FAQ

What is the difference between KOID and ROBO?

KOID is the KraneShares Global Humanoid Robotics and Physical AI Index ETF from KraneShares (humanoid robotics), while ROBO is the ROBO Global Robotics & Automation Index ETF from Exchange Traded Concepts (ROBO Global) (core robotics & automation). They differ in strategy, fees and size.

Which is cheaper, KOID or ROBO?

KOID has the lower expense ratio: 0.69% vs 0.95%.

Which is bigger, KOID or ROBO?

ROBO has more assets: $1.80B vs $319M.
