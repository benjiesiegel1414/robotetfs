The short version

$KOID is cheaper to own, at 0.69% a year versus 0.75%. $ARKQ is the bigger fund, with $1.60B in assets. $ARKQ has been around longer, launching on September 30, 2014.

$ARKQ

ARK's autonomous technology and robotics fund is an actively managed bet on autonomous vehicles, robotics, 3D printing, energy storage and space exploration, run in ARK's high-conviction style. Holdings are concentrated and can include large positions in a few names like electric vehicle and autonomy companies. It behaves more like a growth and disruption fund than a pure robotics index.

$KOID

KraneShares' humanoid robotics fund tracks companies building humanoid robots and the parts inside them: actuators, sensors, reducers, batteries and AI chips. It has grown into one of the largest dedicated humanoid ETFs. Humanoid robots are an early-stage market. Many holdings are component suppliers whose revenue today comes mostly from other businesses, and there is meaningful exposure to Chinese and other Asian companies.

Lower fees and more assets are helpful, but they are not the whole story. The bigger question is which strategy matches what you want to own. This comparison is not a recommendation to buy or sell either fund.

FAQ

What is the difference between ARKQ and KOID?

ARKQ is the ARK Autonomous Technology & Robotics ETF from ARK Invest (core robotics & automation), while KOID is the KraneShares Global Humanoid Robotics and Physical AI Index ETF from KraneShares (humanoid robotics). They differ in strategy, fees and size.

Which is cheaper, ARKQ or KOID?

KOID has the lower expense ratio: 0.69% vs 0.75%.

Which is bigger, ARKQ or KOID?

ARKQ has more assets: $1.60B vs $319M.
