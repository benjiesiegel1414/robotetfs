The short version

$AT is cheaper to own, at 0.69% a year versus 0.89%. $CROB is the bigger fund, with $2.3M in assets. $CROB has been around longer, launching on August 17, 2026.

$AT

Defiance's robotics actuators ETF targets the companies that make actuators and motion components, the parts that turn power into precise robot movement. A narrow component-focused fund launched in 2026. Many holdings serve auto and industrial markets as well as robotics.

$CROB

Defiance's China robotics ETF focuses on Chinese companies building robots, humanoids and automation, a market that leads the world in industrial robot installations. Single-country exposure adds political, regulatory and currency risk on top of the robotics theme.

Lower fees and more assets are helpful, but they are not the whole story. The bigger question is which strategy matches what you want to own. This comparison is not a recommendation to buy or sell either fund.
