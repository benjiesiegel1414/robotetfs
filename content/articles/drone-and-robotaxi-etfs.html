Not every robot works in a factory. Some fly and some drive. Drones and self-driving vehicles are two of the fastest-moving parts of robotics, and a small group of ETFs now targets them directly.

Robotaxis: {{link:CABZ}}

Roundhill's {{link:CABZ}} targets companies building autonomous vehicles and robotaxi services, from vehicle platforms to the sensors, chips and software behind them. It launched on {{inc:CABZ}} and is still small at {{aum:CABZ}}.

Drones: {{link:DRNZ}}

REX's {{link:DRNZ}} focuses on drone and unmanned systems makers. Many of these companies sell heavily to militaries, so contract wins and defense budgets can move the fund. It holds {{aum:DRNZ}}.

The mix: {{link:WDRN}} and {{link:ARKQ}}

WisdomTree's {{link:WDRN}} combines drones with humanoids and other physical AI. ARK's {{link:ARKQ}}, launched back in 2014, is an actively managed fund covering autonomous vehicles, robotics, energy storage and space, run in ARK's concentrated style.

How autonomy funds differ from classic robotics funds

Classic robotics funds like {{link:BOTZ}} and {{link:ROBO}} lean on factory automation, industrial robots and AI chips. Autonomy funds are narrower and more tied to a few big themes: regulation of self-driving cars, defense spending and the pace of commercial drone adoption. Expect bigger swings.
