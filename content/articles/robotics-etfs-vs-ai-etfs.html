AI ETFs and robotics ETFs often show up in the same headlines, and they often hold some of the same stocks. But they are built around different ideas.

The simple difference

AI ETFs focus on intelligence: chips, cloud platforms and software that train and run AI models. Robotics ETFs focus on machines that move: robot arms, humanoids, motion parts, sensors and the automation systems that put AI to work in the physical world.

The overlap is the chips. Robots need AI processors, so the biggest AI chip makers tend to appear in both kinds of funds.

Which way to lean

If you want exposure to the AI build-out broadly, an AI fund covers more of it. If you specifically want the companies building and supplying robots, a robotics or humanoid fund is more direct. Either way, check fees, size and top holdings before you buy. This is not a recommendation.
