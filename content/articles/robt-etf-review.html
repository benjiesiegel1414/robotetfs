{{link:ROBT}} is the First Trust Nasdaq Artificial Intelligence and Robotics ETF, and the short answer to "what am I buying?" is this: 111 stocks spread across AI software, chips and robot makers, with no single position above about 2.4%, for {{exp:ROBT}} a year. It is the most evenly spread way to own the AI and robotics theme, and that is both its strength and its weakness.

I have already done deep dives on BOTZ, ROBO, ARKQ, IBOT, KOID and HUMN. $ROBT keeps coming up in searches next to them, so it gets the same treatment. ROBT at a glance

{{table_of:ROBT,BOTZ,ROBO,AIQ,IBOT}}

$ROBT started trading on Nasdaq on February 21, 2018 at $29.91 a share. On November 19, 2026 it closed at $49.88, with about $645.9 million in net assets and 111 stocks. The basics that matter most:

  • It is an index fund. It tracks the Nasdaq CTA Artificial Intelligence and Robotics Index. No manager picks stocks.
  • It is AI first, robots second. About 53% of the fund is information technology and about 22% is industrials.
  • It is spread thin on purpose. The top 10 holdings were only about 19% of the fund.
  • It is global. About 57% US, then Japan, China, the UK and South Korea.

How ROBT picks its stocks

This is the part most people miss, and it explains everything else about the fund. The index sorts companies into three buckets defined by the Consumer Technology Association (CTA):

BucketWhat it meansIndex weight
EngagersCompanies that design, build or deliver AI or robotics as products, software or systems60%
EnablersCompanies making the building blocks: machinery, autonomous systems, semiconductors, data tools25%
EnhancersCompanies that add AI or robotics services on top of a business that isn't built on them15%

Inside each bucket, every stock gets the same weight. To get in, a company needs at least a $250 million market cap, $3 million a day in trading volume and a 20% free float. The index is rebalanced every quarter and reconstituted twice a year.

Why this matters: Equal weighting means a $5 trillion chip giant and a small robotics maker can sit at nearly the same weight. On November 19, Nvidia was just 0.57% of $ROBT while UBTech Robotics was 1.41%. You get real exposure to smaller names, but you do not get the mega cap winners in size. That cuts both ways, and it shows up in the returns.

How ROBT has performed

$ROBT has almost nine years of history, which is more than most robotics funds. Here is what First Trust reports at NAV through October 30, 2026:

Two things jump out. First, $ROBT has trailed its own index by roughly 0.6% to 1% a year, which is about what you would expect from the fee and trading costs. Second, it has trailed the S&P 500 badly over every period First Trust shows. The five year number is close to flat.

And it did that with more risk. Over three years First Trust shows a standard deviation of 21.04% for $ROBT versus 12.31% for the S&P 500, and a beta of 1.50. You got more volatility and less return. Part of the reason is the equal weighting I described above: when a handful of mega cap tech stocks drive the market, a fund that holds them at under 1% each gets left behind.

My take: A thematic fund has to earn its spot against the boring index fund you already own. Since 2018, $ROBT hasn't. That doesn't mean it can't, but I would not buy it expecting the theme alone to carry the returns.

Does ROBT pay a dividend?

Technically yes, practically no. First Trust's records show a single 2026 distribution of $0.0094 a share, which went ex on June 25 and paid June 30. As of October 30, 2026 the 30-day SEC yield was 0.31% and the 12-month distribution rate was 0.02%. If you want income, this is the wrong fund. If you are an income investor who wants a small growth sleeve, that's fine, just know it won't add to your cash flow.

The real risks

Lagging the market

The biggest risk isn't that $ROBT crashes. It's that it keeps trailing a cheap index fund for years, which is exactly what has happened since 2018.

Volatility

A beta of 1.50 means bigger swings than the market both ways. The closing price ranged from $44.32 to $61.06 over the past 52 weeks.

Theme drift

The fund holds what the index says counts as AI or robotics. Today that includes cybersecurity, chip design software and online platforms. If you came for robots, you may own less of them than you think.

Small and foreign names

Equal weighting pushes money into smaller companies and markets like Japan, Korea and China. Those add currency risk and can be more volatile.

Who ROBT fits, and who it does not

$ROBT may fit you if you want broad, evenly spread exposure to AI and robotics without one stock dominating, you like an index based approach with a long track record, and you're keeping it to a small slice of your portfolio.

$ROBT probably does not fit you if you want a pure robotics fund (look at $ROBO or $BOTZ), the lowest fee (look at $IBOT), heavy exposure to the AI mega caps (look at $AIQ), or any income at all. How I would actually use ROBT

If I owned $ROBT, it would sit next to a broad index fund, not in place of one. The math is simple. A total market fund already gives you Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta and Tesla at full size. $ROBT then adds what the big index barely owns: Ocado, AutoStore, Rainbow Robotics, Fanuc, Symbotic, UBTech and dozens of other automation names at meaningful weights.

Used that way, the equal weighting stops being a weakness and becomes the point. You are not paying 0.65% to own the same mega caps twice. You are paying it to tilt toward the smaller and foreign companies building robots and automation that a cap weighted index mostly ignores.

I would keep it small, something like 5% or less of a portfolio, and I would check it once a year against {{link:ROBO}} and {{link:BOTZ}}. If the gap to the S&P 500 keeps widening, that tells you the theme is not paying for itself, and there is no shame in moving on.

My takeaway

$ROBT is the "own a little of everything" robotics and AI fund. 111 stocks, equal weights inside three buckets, a reasonable 0.65% fee, and almost nine years of history. Its design protects you from any one company blowing up.

The cost of that design is that it has never held the big winners in size, and the numbers show it: about 7.75% a year since launch versus about 14.41% for the S&P 500, with more volatility. I'd rather own it as a small satellite than a core holding, and I'd compare it with $ROBO and $BOTZ before buying.
