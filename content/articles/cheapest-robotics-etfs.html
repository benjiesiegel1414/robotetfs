What the fee gap really costs

On a $10,000 investment, a 0.35% fund costs about $35 a year. A 0.95% fund costs about $95 a year. Over ten years, with growth, that difference can add up to well over $600, before counting any difference in performance.

Why the cheapest fund is not automatically the best

Size matters. Some of the lowest-cost funds are also among the newest and smallest. Small funds can have wider bid-ask spreads, which can cost more than the fee savings if you trade often.

Strategy matters more. A humanoid fund and an industrial automation fund are different bets. Picking the cheapest one only makes sense after you have decided what you want to own.

Big funds are often cheap enough. {{link:BOTZ}} charges {{exp:BOTZ}} with {{aum:BOTZ}} in assets, and {{link:IBOT}} charges {{exp:IBOT}}. Large, established funds tend to trade more easily.
