Humanoid Robot ETFs: Every Fund Betting on Robots That Walk

Humanoid robots went from science projects to one of the hottest themes in the market. Automakers, AI labs and startups are racing to build robots that can walk factory floors and work alongside people, and fund companies followed. There are now 7 humanoid robot ETFs on our list.

What these funds actually own

Very few public companies sell humanoid robots in volume today. So most humanoid ETFs fill their portfolios with the supply chain: makers of actuators and harmonic reducers, precision bearings, motion sensors, batteries, vision systems and the AI chips that run robot brains. Many of those suppliers sit in Japan, China, South Korea and Taiwan.

That means a humanoid ETF today is partly a bet on robots and partly a bet on industrial components and AI hardware. If humanoids scale the way supporters expect, these suppliers could benefit early. If they do not, the funds still own real businesses, but the theme premium can fade.

Each humanoid ETF

1. $KOID: KraneShares Global Humanoid Robotics and Physical AI Index ETF

KraneShares · Index · $332M in assets · 0.69% expense ratio · launched June 4, 2025

KraneShares' humanoid robotics fund tracks companies building humanoid robots and the parts inside them: actuators, sensors, reducers, batteries and AI chips. It has grown into one of the largest dedicated humanoid ETFs.

Worth knowing: Humanoid robots are an early-stage market. Many holdings are component suppliers whose revenue today comes mostly from other businesses, and there is meaningful exposure to Chinese and other Asian companies.

2. $HUMN: Roundhill Humanoid Robotics ETF

Roundhill · $85M in assets · 0.75% expense ratio · launched June 26, 2025

Roundhill's humanoid robotics ETF targets companies directly involved in building humanoid robots and the key components that power them, from robot makers to motion and sensor suppliers.

Worth knowing: A fairly concentrated fund in a very young theme. Expect bigger swings than a broad robotics fund.

3. $BOTT: Themes Humanoid Robotics ETF

Themes ETFs · Index · $59M in assets · 0.35% expense ratio · launched April 22, 2024

Themes ETFs' humanoid robotics fund offers low-cost index exposure to companies tied to humanoid robots, including robot makers and their hardware and AI suppliers.

Worth knowing: One of the cheapest funds on the list. It is still small, so check trading volume and bid-ask spreads before placing larger orders.

4. $CROB: Defiance China Robotics ETF

Defiance · $2.3M in assets · 0.89% expense ratio · launched August 17, 2026

Defiance's China robotics ETF focuses on Chinese companies building robots, humanoids and automation, a market that leads the world in industrial robot installations.

Worth knowing: Single-country exposure adds political, regulatory and currency risk on top of the robotics theme.

5. $AT: Defiance Robotics Actuators ETF

Defiance · $1.8M in assets · 0.69% expense ratio · launched September 14, 2026

Defiance's robotics actuators ETF targets the companies that make actuators and motion components, the parts that turn power into precise robot movement.

Worth knowing: A narrow component-focused fund launched in 2026. Many holdings serve auto and industrial markets as well as robotics.

6. $CBOT: Corgi Robots & Humanoids ETF

Corgi · $500K in assets · 0.35% expense ratio · launched May 6, 2026

Corgi's robots and humanoids ETF is a low-cost fund aimed at companies building robots and humanoid robots.

7. $HOID: Tuttle - Rareview Humanoid Robotics ETF

Rareview Capital · $500K in assets · 0.75% expense ratio · launched October 15, 2026

The Tuttle Capital and Rareview humanoid robotics ETF targets companies tied to the development of humanoid robots, from robot makers to their key suppliers.

Worth knowing: A brand-new fund with almost no history. Data such as AUM may be missing or delayed until it builds a record.

Humanoid ETFs vs. classic robotics ETFs

Classic robotics funds like $BOTZ and $ROBO own the companies already selling industrial robots, surgical systems and factory automation. They have years of history and billions in assets. Humanoid funds are narrower and newer. Some investors pair one of each. For a full breakdown of the biggest humanoid fund, read my KOID ETF review, and for the actively managed alternative, my HUMN ETF review. Either way, compare fees, size and holdings before buying, and remember nothing here is a recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

What is a humanoid robot ETF?

A fund that holds companies building humanoid robots or the parts inside them, such as actuators, reducers, sensors, batteries and AI chips.

What is the largest humanoid robot ETF?

KOID from KraneShares is the largest humanoid-focused ETF on our list. Check the live table for current assets.

Can I buy Tesla Optimus or Figure through an ETF?

Optimus is a Tesla project, so you only get it through funds that hold Tesla. Figure and many other humanoid startups are private, so most ETFs cannot hold them.

Are humanoid ETFs risky?

Yes. Humanoid robots are an early market, most of these funds are new and small, and many hold Asian component suppliers. Expect large price swings.
