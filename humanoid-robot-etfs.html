Humanoid robots went from science projects to one of the hottest themes in the market. Automakers, AI labs and startups are racing to build robots that can walk factory floors and work alongside people, and fund companies followed. There are now 7 humanoid robot ETFs on our list.

What these funds actually own

Very few public companies sell humanoid robots in volume today. So most humanoid ETFs fill their portfolios with the supply chain: makers of actuators and harmonic reducers, precision bearings, motion sensors, batteries, vision systems and the AI chips that run robot brains. Many of those suppliers sit in Japan, China, South Korea and Taiwan.

That means a humanoid ETF today is partly a bet on robots and partly a bet on industrial components and AI hardware. If humanoids scale the way supporters expect, these suppliers could benefit early. If they do not, the funds still own real businesses, but the theme premium can fade.

Each humanoid ETF

1. $KOID: KraneShares Global Humanoid Robotics and Physical AI Index ETF

KraneShares · Index · $322M in assets · 0.69% expense ratio · launched June 4, 2025

KraneShares' humanoid robotics fund tracks companies building humanoid robots and the parts inside them: actuators, sensors, reducers, batteries and AI chips. It has grown into one of the largest dedicated humanoid ETFs.

Worth knowing: Humanoid robots are an early-stage market. Many holdings are component suppliers whose revenue today comes mostly from other businesses, and there is meaningful exposure to Chinese and other Asian companies.

Humanoid ETFs vs. classic robotics ETFs

Classic robotics funds like $BOTZ and $ROBO own the companies already selling industrial robots, surgical systems and factory automation. They have years of history and billions in assets. Humanoid funds are narrower and newer. Some investors pair one of each. Either way, compare fees, size and holdings before buying, and remember nothing here is a recommendation.

Frequently asked questions

What is a humanoid robot ETF?

A fund that holds companies building humanoid robots or the parts inside them, such as actuators, reducers, sensors, batteries and AI chips.

What is the largest humanoid robot ETF?

KOID from KraneShares is the largest humanoid-focused ETF on our list. Check the live table for current assets.

Can I buy Tesla Optimus or Figure through an ETF?

Optimus is a Tesla project, so you only get it through funds that hold Tesla. Figure and many other humanoid startups are private, so most ETFs cannot hold them.

Are humanoid ETFs risky?

Yes. Humanoid robots are an early market, most of these funds are new and small, and many hold Asian component suppliers. Expect large price swings.
